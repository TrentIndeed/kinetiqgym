Modifying the design

Draft. The numbers below are design values for the main build as designed now; they are confirmed or corrected after the prototype is load-tested. The editable CAD is released with the verified build files after that. Until then this page describes the interfaces in words and numbers.

The design is built to be adapted. The two interfaces most builders will change are the battery bay and the motor face. The website adaptation guide illustrates both. This page describes the main build; the budget build runs from the wall and has no battery bay.

Batteries

The reference pack is two 5S LiPo packs in series: 10S, 42 V fully charged, about 30 V empty.

The bay takes two packs stacked, sliding in from the front through a printed cradle:

  • two Thunder Power TP3300-5SR70 (about 141 x 43 x 33 mm each, by the maker's estimate) fill it: the opening is 44 mm, about 0.5 mm a side;
  • two TP2700-5SR70 fit on a printed spacer (a 12 mm block at the front and a 4 mm plate on the outer side), so both pairs sit against the same rear rib.

A 1 mm EVA pad under each cradle roof, a 2 mm separator between the packs, and a 2 mm pad inside the right cover's front wall hold them. The leads leave from the packs' rear end faces into the back step.

To use another pack:

  1. Measure both real packs, including leads and connector bodies: length, width and height. Do not rely on the nominal capacity or the listed size.
  2. Compare them with the bay. Keep room for the pads, the lead bends, the BMS (it hangs from the right cover's ceiling) and a straight removal path through the cradle.
  3. Print the battery fit coupon at your size before you print the end piece and the covers.
  4. Check the electrical system as a whole: - the full-charge voltage must stay well under the ODrive Pro's 58 V, the regulator's 60 V and the fuses' 58 V rating, with room for regenerative braking (the ODrive's over-voltage trip is set at about 45 V); - the BMS must match the series count and chemistry, and pass charge and regen through one port; - recheck the charger, the fuse and wire sizes, and the regen current limit: it may not exceed the pack's charge rating (39.6 A for the TP3300 pair, 32.4 A for the TP2700 pair).

A pack that fits is not automatically electrically compatible.

Motors

The drive is direct: the motor turns the drum through a rigid clamp coupling, with no belt or gear between them. Both builds use an off-the-shelf motor, the Freerchobby MP8085 50 Kv: no custom windings.

The motor face plate (0.250 in 6061) stands on two tabs in slots in the base plate and is bolted to the base and rear plates into nuts in its windows. It carries the MP8085's pattern, 4 x M5 on Ø56 mm on the 45° diagonals, and a Ø16 mm shaft hole. A different motor needs its own pattern on the same centerline (the drum axis), clear of the existing holes and the nut windows.

How to judge a motor:

  • Cable force ≈ motor torque ÷ drum rope radius (12.7 mm on the main build's 7/8 in drum). 220 lb (979 N) needs about 12.4 N·m.
  • Current ≈ torque ÷ torque constant, with the torque constant about 8.27 ÷ Kv N·m per amp. At 50 Kv, 12.4 N·m takes about 75 A: inside the ODrive Pro's 80 A. A smaller drum lowers the current but raises the felt mass.
  • Felt inertia: through the 12.7 mm radius, a rotor's inertia J (kg·m²) feels like about 6,200 x J kg at the rope. A lighter rotor feels better; the firmware can cancel part of it, but not all. The 10 lb minimum should feel like 10 lb: keep the felt mass under about 6.4 kg before compensation (no more than about 30 % for the firmware to cancel).
  • Heat: the rating is held through a 20-30 s set. Check the motor's copper loss at that current and how many sets it takes to reach the thermistor's limit.
  • Prefer low-voltage (48 V or less) DC brushless motors with a high pole count, close to the reference size. Avoid hub motors (their rotors feel like a flywheel) and high-voltage AC servos: at this torque they are far larger and heavier than the body, or they need a reduction stage, which spoils the back-drive feel.
  • A larger motor or a different Kv does not by itself make a safe resistance rating. The shared parts (frame, drum, level-wind, exit and rack side) are sized by calculation for 220 lb, and the drum and level-wind for 234 lb (the most the ODrive's 80 A can pull); nothing is checked above that, and none of it is tested yet.

After a motor change, update the motor, encoder, current and thermal settings before any powered test (firmware-setup.md).

A knob instead of the touch screen

The budget build already does this: an EC11 rotary encoder with a push button and a flush start/stop button, and a phone app (Bluetooth, through a XIAO ESP32-C3) handles the settings. On the main build the same change closes the screen window and drops the screen and its frame. Whether the Raspberry Pi can also be left out depends on the control software, which is not released yet.
