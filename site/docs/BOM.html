Draft reference · October 2026

Bill of materials

Draft. This is the parts list for the prototype being built now (October 2026). It is confirmed, with exact hardware counts, in the verified release after the prototype has been load-tested. Links are examples checked in October 2026; prices and stock change. Nothing here is endorsed by the suppliers.

The website budget has the same list with dated prices, per-build totals and editable prices.

How the list is organized. The main list is what a builder has to buy, in order of importance, from motor and controller to adhesives. It assumes you make the metal and printed parts yourself (laser cutting or CNC, a drill with taps, and a 3D printer). The accessory list is what many builders already have. The last section gives typical costs for having the metal made.

What the builds share. Every build uses the same bolted 6061 aluminum frame, drum, level-wind, exit and rack side. The shared parts are sized for the 220 lb peak builds: in the load study every member passes 1.5x at 220 lb, to be confirmed by load testing. Direct drive only: the motor turns the drum through a clamp coupling, with no belt or gear between them.

C80100 220 lb peak APS C80100, wound to about 25 Kv ODrive Pro 10S battery 429 x 227 x 103 mm
  • Main: the reference build. A light rotor (about 2.5 kg felt at the rope before compensation), about 13 N·m.
  • Lite: the low-cost build on the main build's frame, mount and motor body, plugged into the wall. A Mean Well 36 V, 600 W supply inside, an ODrive S1 with two brake resistors on the base plate, and a phone app (Bluetooth) and a knob in place of the battery, the Raspberry Pi and the touchscreen. At about 25 Kv, 158 lb takes 37-45 A.
  • MP80100: the same frame with a 44 mm longer motor bay. About 4 kg felt at the rope; ships from China.
  • MP80100 wye: if the 50 Kv motor is wound delta, re-terminating it in wye gives about 29 Kv, so 220 lb takes about 60 A (the ODrive Pro gives 70 A continuous). Ask the seller how it is wound before buying for 220 lb.
  • C80100: wound to order at about 25 Kv, so 220 lb takes about 52-63 A on the ODrive Pro. The motor is 15 mm shorter than the MP80100.

No CNC? Have the metal made

Typical costs for having the metal parts made for one machine: about $290-505 in all. Prices depend on the shop, the quantity and the lead time, so get quotes. This replaces the aluminum line in the main list. Ready-made metal kits are planned after testing.

Item Typical cost (USD) Notes
Laser-cut plate set, 6061-T6 200-300 The base, rear and motor face plates, the rack cheeks and the drum flanges, plus the four 2 mm steel bearing cheeks, from an instant-priced laser service. The prototype's plates came to about $200-300 at SendCutSend (October 2026). Tapping and countersinking are extra, charged per hole.
Thick plate parts: drum holders, corner gussets and the mating plate 75-175 The two 3/8 in drum bearing holders, the three 1/4 in corner gussets and the 3/4 in mating plate, cut from plate like the rest (no machined blocks). The 28 mm bearing bores are finished to H7 and the M10 threads tapped after cutting.
Drum tube, 6061, 1-1/4 in OD x 0.250 in wall, 100 mm 15-30 From a local metal shop, cut square; ask for T6.

The holes in the plate edges and the tube ends are drilled and tapped by hand through the printed drill guides.

Printed parts

Printed for every build (materials and settings in printing.md):

  • PAHT-CF: the 81T drum gear, the 45T screw gear, the traveller frame, the fixed roller bracket, the top tie, the far-end screw stand, the screw-end tower, the rope clamp ring, the exit bearing housing, the exit carrier, its retainers and rail caps.
  • PA-CF: the motor-end and battery-end pieces, the ODrive bracket, the latch finger bar and the service lid.
  • PETG-CF: the two covers (end caps printed in) and the rear I/O panel.
  • PETG: the screen frame, the front board plate and its standoffs, the WAGO bracket, the fan duct, and the TP2700 battery spacer (only with the TP2700 pair).
  • TPU 95A or EVA foam: the three battery pads.
  • PETG or PLA: the five drill guides for the edge holes (tools, not parts of the machine).

The Lite adds a printed tray for its power supply and leaves out the battery parts, the screen frame and the rear I/O panel.
