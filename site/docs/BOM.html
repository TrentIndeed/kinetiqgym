Draft reference · October 2026

Bill of materials

Draft. This is the parts list for the builds as designed now (October 2026). It is confirmed, with exact hardware counts, in the verified release after a prototype has been load-tested. Links are examples checked in October 2026; prices and stock change. Nothing here is endorsed by the suppliers.

The website budget has the same list with dated prices, per-build totals and editable prices.

How the list is organized. The main list is what a builder has to buy, in order of importance, from motor and controller to hardware and metal stock. Fasteners and small bulk parts are counted by the pieces used; the note gives the pack. The accessory list is what many builders already have. The last section gives typical costs for having the main build's metal made.

Ratings. Each rating is a design target held through a 20-30 s set (with about 3 minutes' rest), not yet verified. The motor's torque against current and the controllers' current ratings are still to be bench-tested, and nothing has been load-tested. The 10 lb minimum is designed to feel like 10 lb: the drums are small and light, and the firmware cancels part of the motor's inertia (about 29 % of the felt mass on the main build, 24 % on the budget build).

  • Main: the bolted 6061 aluminum frame, the 10S battery and the touchscreen. The drum is 7/8 in OD x 1/8 in wall 6061 tube used as bought: 220 lb takes about 75 A from the ODrive Pro. 2.6 m of pull. A machined heatsink and a 40 mm fan cool the ODrive, and a thermistor on the motor's stator lets it roll the current back when the motor runs hot. In the load study every part passes 1.5x: the frame and rack side at 220 lb (the lowest, the rack side's pad bar, at about 1.6x), the level-wind and drum at 234 lb, the most the ODrive's 80 A can pull.
  • Budget: its own simpler frame, cut with a hacksaw and drilled through printed guides, hung on the rack upright with one hitch pin. The motor sits in an outside pod and turns a 1 in drum tube on a 20 mm shaft through a steel clamp coupling. 2.0 m of pull. 180 lb takes about 69 A from the ODESC, whose 70 A rating is the seller's and unverified: bench-test it first (if it only holds 53 A, the build tops out near 138 lb). A knob, a start/stop button and a phone app (Bluetooth) take the place of the battery and the screen. In the load study every structural part and bolt passes 1.5x at 180 lb.
  • Wall: for people without a rack: it bolts to wall studs or concrete. It is in design; its parts list follows once the CAD is done.

Direct drive only: the motor turns the drum through a clamp coupling, with no belt or gear between them. Nothing is welded or turned on a lathe: round parts are bought at their final size and cut to length. Every load-bearing joint is a bolt and a nut.

No CNC? Have the metal made

Main build. Typical costs for having the metal parts made for one machine: about $310-545 in all. Prices depend on the shop, the quantity and the lead time, so get quotes. This replaces the aluminum line in the main list. Ready-made metal kits are planned after testing. The budget build needs no CNC: its plate and angle are bought near size and cut with a hacksaw.

Drum tube, 6061-T6, 7/8 in OD x 1/8 in wall, 162 mm 10-20 From a metal supplier such as Metal Supermarkets or Online Metals, cut square (reference: about $8.50 a foot). Used as bought: no lathe.

The holes in the plate edges are drilled by hand through the printed drill guides, into nut windows cut through the plates. The drum tube's end notches are sawn and filed through a printed tube-end guide.
