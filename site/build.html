Make it yours

Change the interfaces.
Keep the system in view.

The design is built to be adapted: the battery bay and the motor face are the two interfaces most builders will change. The editable CAD comes with the verified release.

Measure the pack, then the bay.

  1. Measure the real pack’s length, width and height, including leads and connectors.
  2. Compare it with the bay: it takes two 5S packs of the TP3300 size, or two of the TP2700 size on a printed spacer.
  3. Keep space for padding, lead bends, the BMS and a straight removal path through the cradle.
  4. Reassess the series count, BMS, charger and regenerative current limits before any powered test.

The reference uses two 5S packs in series (10S, 42 V fully charged). A pack that fits is not automatically electrically compatible.

Preserve the shaft centerline.

  1. Measure shaft diameter and projection, pilot diameter, bolt pattern, body diameter and length.
  2. The motor face plate carries two bolt circles (Ø56 and Ø59 mm) and a Ø16 shaft hole. A new motor needs its own pattern on the same centerline.
  3. Match the coupling’s motor-side bore, and check the covers and the phase-lead exit against the new motor body.
  4. Recalculate torque-to-cable force and update the motor, encoder, current and thermal settings before testing.

The drive is direct: cable force ≈ motor torque ÷ effective drum radius. A different Kv or a larger motor does not by itself make a safe resistance rating.

Open hardware, once it is proven

Open to build.
The name stays with the maker.

Kinetiq Gym is released as open hardware once the design is verified on the physical prototype. Anyone will be able to build it, change it and share their changes under the licences below.

Until then the public repository holds this website, the documentation and the renders. Contributions open with the verified release: there is no tested design to contribute to yet.
